How it works

Three moves, repeated until the work is small.

move 1

Say what kind of thing it is

A Goal, a Project, a Challenge, a Problem, a Decision, an Opportunity or a Risk. The kind changes the questions you are asked and the ways of thinking you are offered.

move 2

Cut it into steps

By hand, or by picking a way of thinking — it proposes a cut and nothing is added until you have looked at it. Open any step and it becomes a whole board of its own.

move 3

Do the list

Any step that cannot sensibly be cut smaller gets marked as an action, and every action collects in one flat list. 3 of 11 things done — the counts, not just a bar.

Seven kinds of thing

Not everything breaks down the same way.

A goal is not a problem, and a decision is not a risk. Greggle asks what kind of thing you are working on first, because the kind decides what good questions look like.

a Goal

Something you are aiming at, without a fixed end date.

say… Make the first team this season.

a Project

Something you are setting out to do that finishes.

say… The science fair project.

a Challenge

Something difficult to win, where the outcome is not yours to control.

say… The debating final.

a Problem

Something that has gone wrong and needs diagnosing.

say… The band keeps missing practice.

a Decision

A choice between options.

say… Which subjects to take next year.

an Opportunity

Something that might be worth pursuing, before you have decided to.

say… A stall at the Saturday market.

a Risk

Something that could go wrong, and has not yet.

say… What if the venue cancels?

Fourteen ways of thinking

None of them were invented here. That is the point.

These are the methods factories, consultancies and labs have used for decades — the ones normally locked inside textbooks and training courses. Greggle lays them out on your board and walks you through them. Pick one and it proposes a cut; nothing is added until you have reviewed it.

5 Whys

Began on Toyota’s factory floor in the 1950s

Ask why of the answer, five times, until you reach a cause you can actually act on.

Working Backwards

How Amazon starts new products

Define what done looks like and the evidence that would prove it, then walk back to now.

Pre-mortem

Psychologist Gary Klein’s insurance against wishful thinking

It is twelve months from now and this failed. Work out why, then prevent it.

First Principles

Aristotle’s method

Find the truths nothing else rests on, then build up from them alone.

Force Field Analysis

Psychologist Kurt Lewin, 1940s

What is pushing for the change, what is holding it back, and which single restraint you could actually remove.

Options and Criteria

The decision matrix, kept honest

Write down what a good answer would have to do before you look at the answers.

Then it checks

It reads your work back and says what is missing.

One button reads every step in a branch and reports what is undecided, what was asked and never answered, and what stops short of an answer. A percentage on its own flatters a plan, so the counts are always shown beside it.

Yours

No account. Nothing leaves your device.

There is nothing to sign up for. The app’s security policy blocks the internet outright and a test proves it makes no connections. Your work lives in your browser and in a file you own — the file is how it moves to another device.
